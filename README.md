# Introductory Statistics (AI Plus One)

## About the book

This compact rough draft is for first college statistics readers who know basic algebra, fractions and tables. A fictional bus service gives you a recurring question: what can these arrival records actually support? You will describe variation, reason about sampling, and explain the limits of an estimate or a rollout recommendation. Paper or a spreadsheet is enough; Python 3.10+ is optional.

## Start reading

Start with [Ask what the data can represent](chapters/01-representation.md). Use the [complete chapter guide](#chapter-guide) to find every chapter in reading order.

## Chapter guide

### Part 1 · Build a useful description

1. [Ask what the data can represent](chapters/01-representation.md): Define the unit, the population and what was actually sampled.
2. [Summarize without hiding variation](chapters/02-description.md): Use counts and distribution displays without hiding long delays or uneven coverage.

### Part 2 · Reason beyond the observed rows

3. [Use sampling variation](chapters/03-sampling-variation.md): Explain why estimates change across samples under a specified process.
4. [Estimate and test with uncertainty](chapters/04-inference.md): Build and interpret an interval or test in terms of its assumptions and uncertainty.
5. [Model relationships carefully](chapters/05-relationships.md): Separate a fitted relationship from evidence of cause and check the scope of prediction.

## How to teach or study it

Decide what the table can represent before computing anything. Read distributions before interpreting sample-to-sample variation, then use that variation to make and qualify an inference. Make a first attempt on paper. In the [unaided capstone](practice/capstone.md), submit a recommendation supported by the synthetic arrivals; distinguish operational and causal questions. Broader modeling and expert review remain open.
